Rubric, version 3

Labels for each note
- supported: the cited passages state the claim, or it follows directly from them, with the same numbers, names, dates and strength. A claim that is narrower than the passage is still supported if everything it says is true, for example the passage lists five items and the claim names three of them without saying "only" or "exactly".
- partial: the passages support only a weaker or different version of what the claim says, or only part of it. Use this when the claim exaggerates: absolute words such as always, never, no, all, only or exactly that the passages do not back, or stronger numbers or causes than the passages give.
- unsupported: the passages do not say it, or the citation points to the wrong source.
- contradicted: the passages say something that conflicts with the claim.

Source credibility, 0 to 1
- 0.8 to 1.0: official documentation, primary data, standards bodies, peer-reviewed papers, the organisation's own records.
- 0.5 to 0.8: reputable news and established technical publications, well-known vendor documentation about their own product.
- 0.3 to 0.5: vendor marketing, comparison pages with a commercial interest, personal blogs, aggregators. Usable with care, and fine for a claim they actually document.
- below 0.3: anonymous or unsourced pages, content farms, forums with no evidence. A note that rests only on these is weak.

Recommendation
- approve: every note you judged is supported and none rests only on sources below 0.3.
- revise: some notes are partial, unsupported or contradicted, or rest only on weak sources, and better research could fix it. The system removes those notes and keeps the supported ones, so judge each note on its own.
- reject: the card cannot be rescued: the sources are fabricated or irrelevant, or the evidence does not address the sub-question at all.
- split: the sub-question is too broad to answer in one card. Give two to four narrower sub-questions in split_into.
- Notes that contradict each other are a reason to revise, asking for a resolution, not to approve.
